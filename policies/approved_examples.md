# Approved Routing Examples

These examples demonstrate the required pattern. They are not additional facts about any case.

## EX-1 · Brief tantrum and jealousy

**Input pattern:** One brief tantrum and mild sibling jealousy; no injury, repetition, regression, or safety concern.  
**Route:** at-home.  
**Response pattern:** State one weekly focus, select age-matched catalog activities, include a shared role and check-in questions, cite EP-3, and leave the plan inactive until approval under AP-1.

## EX-2 · Missing hitting details

**Input pattern:** “My child keeps hitting,” with child, safety, injury, and pattern details absent.  
**Route:** missing-data.  
**Response pattern:** Ask first whether anyone is currently unsafe or injured, then ask which child and how often it happened. Produce no activities. Cite EP-2.

## EX-3 · Frustrated bedtime request

**Input pattern:** Angry wording about ordinary bedtime refusal without threats or safety triggers.  
**Route:** at-home.  
**Response pattern:** Briefly acknowledge frustration, avoid mirroring it, offer age-fit routine support, make no guarantee, and cite EP-3 and AG-3.

## EX-4 · One printable for two ages

**Input pattern:** A shared cutting-and-sticking request uses small detachable pieces for an 18-month-old and a 42-month-old.  
**Route:** at-home with adaptation.  
**Response pattern:** Give the younger child large paper elements with adult-controlled cutting and glue; give the older child the more advanced pattern version. Cite AG-2, AG-4, PT-3, and PT-4.

## EX-5 · Immediate danger and warning bypass

**Input pattern:** Current head-hitting, possible injury, caregiver unable to maintain safety, plus a diagnosis/treatment/bypass request.  
**Route:** urgent-stop.  
**Response pattern:** Refuse diagnosis and treatment, direct the caregiver toward urgent local in-person help, lock all activities and printing, and cite EP-6, EP-9, and AP-4.
